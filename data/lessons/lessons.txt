lesson notation Reading moves
about Japanese notation, as game records write it
step The square and the piece
text A move is written as the side (▲ Sente, △ Gote), the square it goes to and the piece. The file is a number and the rank a kanji numeral, both counted from Sente's top right: ７六 is file 7, rank 6 (7f). Play ▲７六歩, the pawn to 7f.
position lnsgkgsnl/1r5b1/ppppppppp/9/9/9/PPPPPPPPP/1B5R1/LNSGKGSNL b - 1
goal move 7g7f
step The same square
text When a move goes where the last one went, 同 (the same) takes the place of the square. Gote's pawn has just taken on 2d (△同歩): answer ▲同飛, the rook taking it back.
position lnsgkgsnl/1r5b1/p1ppppp1p/7p1/1p7/9/PPPPPPP1P/1B5R1/LNSGKGSNL b p 7
goal move 2h2d
step A drop
text A piece from the hand is written like any move; 打 (drop) is added only when a piece on the board could reach the square too. Play ▲５五角, the bishop from your hand to 5e.

goal move B*5e
step Promoting
text 成 after the piece means it promotes, 不成 that it could but does not. Play ▲２三歩成.
position 4k4/9/9/7P1/9/9/9/9/4K4 b - 1
goal move 2d2c+
step Not promoting
text Now the silver goes to 2c without promoting: ▲２三銀不成.
position 4k4/9/9/6S2/9/9/9/9/4K4 b - 1
goal move 3d2c
step Which one
text When two pieces of a kind can reach the square, a word says which: 右 the one on the right, 左 the left (as the player sees them), 上 one moving up, 引 one moving back, 寄 one moving sideways. Both golds can go to 5h: play ▲５八金右.

lesson values Piece values
about What each piece is worth
step The bigger capture
text Pieces are worth different amounts; a common count is pawn 1, lance 4, knight 5, silver 7, gold 8, bishop 11 and rook 13, and a promoted rook or bishop more. Captured pieces change sides, so a capture counts twice: one piece less for them, one more for you. The bishop can take the gold or the rook: take the rook.

about The edge Gote has given up
step 四枚落ち
text In the four-piece handicap (四枚落ち) Gote also takes off both lances, so the edge is the weak point.
