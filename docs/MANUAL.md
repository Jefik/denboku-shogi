# Denboku Shōgi manual

How to use the game on the Kindle, Linux, Windows and Android. Installing is in [`INSTALL.md`](INSTALL.md); the rules of shōgi and of each variant are in the game, under ☰ → Help → How to Play.

## The screen

- One layout for every screen. Portrait: the moves at the top (more rows on taller screens; a single row has ⌄ at its end, which opens them all as a list), a panel for each player (name, clock and the pieces in hand) above and below the board, and a bar of actions at the bottom. Landscape: the board at the left, its full height, and a column at the right with the opponent's panel, the moves, your panel and the bar; the column stops growing on wide windows and the rest goes around.
- The bar: ☰ (the menu), Undo (Takeback in network games), Hint (the chat in network games, in the same place, with a dot when a line came), ◀ ▶ through the moves and ⓘ (the game's players, clock and handicap, and in standard shōgi the strategies up to the move shown, as players name them: each side's rook, Static Rook (居飛車) or which Ranging Rook (四間飛車, 中飛車…), the castle its king sits in (Mino, Yagura, Boat, Bear in the hole…) and the Bishop Exchange). Each player's panel has the name at the left and the clock at the right; the side to move gets a pill with its clock, or "To move" in games without one. A check and the result show with the moves, and so does a note when the other player of a network game has left.
- ☰ opens the menu as a sheet of large tiles, from the bottom in portrait and over the column at the right in landscape (wider, over the board, when the window is narrow): Games, Replay and Help open a page of their own, with ‹ to go back. ×, a tap outside it, Escape or a drag towards the edge it came from closes it.
- Dialogs open as drawers over the game, which does not move: at the bottom in portrait, over the column in landscape. Those that keep the game in sight or in use (Preferences, to see its looks change, the game over notice, the network lobby, Analysis, the Sandbox's pieces in hand) open beside it instead: under the board in portrait, at the right of the column in landscape. Drawers and the ☰ menu are all as wide as each other (at most 460 px in landscape, 720 px centered in portrait), and on a wide screen they stay by the game, not at the window's edge. Every page of the ☰ menu is as large as its first. The drawers over the game are as wide, and those without buttons at their foot (How to Play, About…) at least as tall; all have the same head: title, round ‹ and ×. Short choices are chips; a longer list opens over it, one choice per row (the piece sets with their pieces).
- A long page scrolls with a drag. On the Kindle it turns instead, as the Kindle's own lists: a swipe up shows the next page, down the one before (Preferences → Appearance → Turn pages in long lists, also on Android for e-ink devices). Scrolling on the Kindle moves at the e-ink's pace, about three steps a second.
- ×, a tap outside a drawer, Escape or a drag on its title towards the edge closes it as Cancel does. A tap outside, though, keeps the choice just made in a list (a piece set, a sound, the language) as Confirm does, and leaves alone the pages that wait on something (the network lobby and waits, a Lishogi challenge). A tap on a button of the bar beside it (☰, Hint…) closes it and presses the button, except the chat's, which leaves it open. A page opened from another has ‹ to go back to it, and one opened from the ☰ menu ‹ back to the menu; a drawer opened otherwise has only ×. With the keyboard or a controller, going back puts the cursor on the row or tile that opened the page. A drag in a drawer or in a menu too long for the screen scrolls it, and one let go while moving glides on, slowing down (on the Kindle it goes straight to where it would stop).
- Network and Lishogi games have a chat, with Lishogi's quick lines under its field against a person (HI, GL, HF, U2, YORO before the third move; GG, WP, TY, BYE after the game; in Japanese こん, よろしく and おつ, あり, また; each once, two at most). In landscape it sits against the board's left side, and the chat button shows or hides it. In portrait the chat button opens it as a drawer under the board (over the whole screen above the keyboard while the Kindle's or the phone's on-screen keyboard is up), and ×, the button or a drag down closes it. In the lobby (and beside a result notice) the chat takes taps too; in portrait the lobby's Chat row opens it above the lobby. Its lines go when the game is left; a Lishogi game's come back from Lishogi when the game opens again (the app restarted, the game continued).
- On computers the game opens maximized.
- The move list: tap a move to see that position, drag it to scroll; ▶ up to the last move, or End, goes back to the game.
- The screen turns with the device unless Preferences → Appearance → Screen fixes it. On the Kindle it is portrait, landscape or landscape upside down (the Kindle does not turn portrait upside down).

## Playing

- Tap a piece, then one of the marked squares, or with a mouse drag it there. Pieces in hand are dropped the same way. When a move can promote, the game asks, unless automatic promotion is on in Preferences → Game. In Kyotoshōgi a piece in hand can be dropped either side up, and the game asks which.
- During the opponent's turn you can already pick your next move (a premove): as on Lishogi, a piece may be premoved anywhere it reaches on an empty board (past a piece that may move away, onto one of yours that may be taken), and the move is played if it is then legal. A piece picked without a target stays picked when the opponent moves, unless they took it.
- Plan arrows and circles: on touch screens drag from square to square for an arrow, or hold a square for a circle; with a mouse, the same with the right button.
- Undo takes back the last move (against the computer, your move and its reply). Hint shows a good move from the engine as a blue arrow (a circle for a drop), until a move is made.
- When the game is over, the result notice (rematch, analysis, new game) can be closed and brought back with ☰ → Result; opening Preferences over it leaves it there.
- ☰ → Resign ends the game. ☰ → Declare Impasse (入玉宣言) claims a win by the impasse rule (the Japan Shōgi Association's 27-point rule, explained in How to Play), in the variants that have it. It is not offered in network or online games, where the other side would not hear of it. If the position does not allow it yet, it says what is needed.
- When a game ends, its result shows beside the board with Analyze Game (when the game can be analyzed), New Game, Close and Rematch. Rematch, the main button, plays the same game again; against the engine the sides are swapped, unless there is a handicap. The moves can still be browsed and the menu used while it is there.
- ☰ → Flip Board turns the board around.
- ☰ → Quit, or the window's close button, asks first; the game in progress is kept for next time (in an online game it also offers to resign). The close button pressed again while it asks quits at once.
- Cancel in a dialog opened from ☰ goes back to the menu.
- On computers the whole game also plays from the keyboard or a game controller (Xbox and other XInput controllers, DirectInput and plain USB ones, PlayStation and Switch controllers on Linux), while the game's window is the active one; behind another window the controller's presses are left to that one.
- On the board: the arrow keys or the d-pad show a cursor and move it. The controller follows the Japanese convention, by position. The right face button (Xbox B, Nintendo A, PlayStation ○) or Enter picks up and places a piece, and answers the promotion question (← → choose). The bottom one (Xbox A, Nintendo B, ×) or Escape puts it back. The left one or Tab goes through the pieces in hand, and the top one or H gives a hint.
- LB/RB (Page Up/Down) go through the moves, and keep going while held. LT undoes (Ctrl+Z) and LS, pressing the left stick, redoes (Ctrl+Y). RT or M browses the moves: the arrows then go back (↑ ←) and forward (↓ →) through the move list, and Home and End go to the first and the last move. They do the same after a move is picked in the list with the mouse. Select, Cancel or an action on the game (the hand, Hint, Undo, Redo) goes back to the board.
- Start (Menu, +, Options), F10, or Escape when there is nothing to put back opens ☰. Select (View, −, Create) or Ctrl+, opens Preferences. RS or F flips the board, and F11 is full screen, with a menu or a page open too. Pushing the right stick up or down scrolls an open page or the menu.
- In the ☰ menu the d-pad or the arrows move between the tiles (up from the first row to ‹ and ×). Confirm or Enter opens a page or picks the tile, cancel or Escape goes back a page or closes the menu, and Start or F10 closes it too.
- In a dialog a frame marks the field in use. Up and down go from field to field (inside a list or a long text they move in it); left and right change a drop-down, a number or a slider. Confirm or Enter opens a drop-down or presses the button, chip or switch in the frame (only the Start button starts a game). In a list of choices the arrows only move, confirm chooses a row (shown at once), and confirm again on the chosen row keeps it and closes the list. Cancel or Escape goes back a page (the cursor returns to the row it left) or closes the dialog, and Start or F10 closes every page at once. The page scrolls with the cursor. LB/RB or Ctrl+Page Up/Down go to the section of Preferences before or after (in Preferences → Variants, the variant before or after), or to the chip before or after on a page with a row of them (the variants in Rules). Select closes Preferences.
- While you play this way, small icons in the corners of the board, of the hand of the side to move and of the move list show the key or button for each, the one in use brighter. A click or tap hides them all.
- Preferences → Controls lists every action with its keys and controller buttons; a tap opens the action with its two keys and two buttons: press one, then the new key or button (Clear leaves it empty; a key or button set here leaves the action it had). Restore defaults puts them all back. Controller buttons picks the names and colors shown (Xbox, Nintendo or PlayStation; Automatic follows the controller connected), and a switch turns the keys and buttons beside the buttons on screen off. The page also shows the controller connected.

## New Game

☰ → New Game… asks:

- Players: Computer, Two players (on one device), Engines (engine vs engine, each side at its own level; Pause, where Hint is in other games, stops and resumes it), Network, Lishogi or CSA (a CSA server).
- You play: Sente (▲, moves first), Gote (△) or a random side.
- Engine: level 1 to 10 (Fairy-Stockfish).
- Clock: none, a preset, or Custom (minutes per player, byoyomi and increment in seconds).
- Fields for a link, a username or the Lishogi token have a Paste button beside them (not on the Kindle), which on the phone takes Android's clipboard. On the phone a long press on any text field brings Android's text toolbar (Paste, Select all, and Cut and Copy when text is selected; it also comes up by itself once text is selected), and the keyboard's own Paste works too. On computers the right button gives GTK's menu.
- On Lishogi the choices are Lishogi's own: the opponent (anyone, the Lishogi AI at level 1 to 10, a user, a friend by link, waiting for or accepting a challenge, or continuing a game in progress, one started on the site included: the game's link, or none for your game in progress; Lishogi lets an app play only games with enough time, see below); the time control (real time, correspondence with days per move, or unlimited; looking for anyone is in real time only), with Lishogi's lists of time per player, byoyomi, increment and byoyomi periods; the rating range of a seek (up to 750 points below and above yours); rated or not; and a handicap against the AI or in a challenge. Rated is off where Lishogi would make the game casual anyway: against the AI, with a handicap, unlimited time, more than one byoyomi period, or byoyomi and increment together; a rated seek plays a random side. The game says before sending what Lishogi would refuse: apps may only play games of at least 5 minutes by Lishogi's estimate, 15 when rated.
- Handicap: even, or the official 駒落ち (lance up to 10 pieces); the giving side (上手) moves first. Standard shōgi only; when hosting a network game it is under "More options", with the move rules.
- Variant: Standard, Minishōgi, Kyotoshōgi, Checkshōgi, Dōbutsu, Goro Goro (5×6, the Japan Shōgi Association's beginners' shōgi), Judkins shōgi (6×6, Minishōgi's pieces and a knight, a two-rank zone), Euroshōgi (8×8, a knight that also steps sideways, promotion forced in three ranks) or Tori shōgi (7×7 with birds, the 1-kanji Noto Sans JP set and move marks on by default; the left and right quails, both 鶉, carry 左 or 右 on a small red seal as real sets do, or face away from their side in the animal and pictorial sets). Rules explains each, on the same page as How to Play's Variants: a chip per variant, the one shown named in the title. Lishogi plays Standard, Minishōgi, Kyotoshōgi, Checkshōgi and Dōbutsu; CSA servers the standard game.
- Legal moves and Illegal moves: whether legal moves are marked, and whether an illegal move is refused or allowed and loses, as over a real board; the end of the game then says which rule it broke.

## Records

- ☰ → Games → Save Game and Save as CSA write the game as KIF or CSA (not again if the same game in the same position is already saved), with a passing notice; Load Game lists the saved games: a tap opens one, holding it deletes it (after asking), and Delete All removes them all.
- ☰ → Games → Game History lists every finished game (date, variant, players, result and length), newest first, the same way; it keeps the last 200.
- News that needs no answer (a game saved or copied, nothing to load) shows for a few seconds at the top of the board.
- ☰ → Games → Copy Game (KIF) and Paste Game use the clipboard (computers only), so Lishogi's import page and other apps can read the game.
- ☰ → Games → Open from Lishogi… loads a Lishogi game by its link or ID.
- ☰ → Analyze Game has the engine look at every position of a finished game (or a loaded one, or one between two players here), about a second each, while you go on browsing. Moves that lost winning chances get ?! (inaccuracy), ? (mistake) or ?? (blunder) in the move list and on their square, as on Lishogi. The move list shows the evaluation after the shown move (+ is good for sente, #3 a mate in 3) and puts it in words as Japanese commentary does: 互角 even within 3 pawns, then 有利 slightly better, 優勢 better past 8, 勝勢 winning past 20 or with a mate. It also says what a marked move was and the best move instead, and the board shows the engine's best moves from the shown position as blue arrows (the thickest is the best).
- The Analysis dialog has the graph of the game (a tap goes to that move), the time each move took, and for each side the marks, the average centipawn loss and lichess's accuracy; a tap on a count goes to that side's next move with that mark. Its Options, as in lichess's analysis menu, open a page over it (‹ goes back): time per move (1 to 10 s), CPUs and memory (computers only), lines (1 to 5 arrows), best move arrows and marks on the board. Analyze again uses the new time, CPUs, memory and lines. ☰ → Analysis opens the dialog again, and Stop ends the analysis early. Nothing is kept once the game changes.
- ☰ → Replay plays the game back: fast, slow, or in real time (the time each move took). While it plays, the bar's Undo and Hint become its speed (fast, slow, real time) and Pause/Resume; stepping through the moves pauses it, and a tap on the board stops it.
- A loaded game is shown between two players.
- The game in progress is saved on every move and comes back the next time the game opens.

## Tutorial

☰ → Help → Tutorial has short lessons, in chapters: the board and each piece, promotion, captures and drops (二歩 and the other drops not allowed), check and checkmate (打ち歩詰め) with the head, belly and tail golds, a sacrifice, mates against the Mino and the Silver crown (mates in two: every move checks and the other side defends) and brinkmate (必至, a quiet move that leaves no defence), squares and the pieces' kanji (tap the square named, or the piece), reading Japanese notation (▲７六歩, 同, 打, 成 and 不成, 右 and 左: the move written is played on the board), and then strategy: principles (居玉は避けよ, the king off its starting square; 玉飛接近すべからず, the king away from the rook; speed before pieces at the end), castles (the Mino family, Yagura, Boat, Bear in the hole on either side), openings (Climbing Silver, Double Wing Attack, Double Yagura, Ranging Rook and others), piece values, two tesuji and the book lines of the two- and four-piece handicaps; every variant has lessons of its own after those. The list opens with a proverb of the day (格言), one of 25, with what it means. The first start, with no game saved, asks once whether to begin with it.

A lesson is played on the board, its step in a drawer beside it (under the game in portrait): what to do, its moves, and Again, Hint and Skip (Next once the step is done). A step is passed or not, with no score: a wrong move is not played and the drawer says why (a silver does not step sideways, 二歩, a king left in check…), and the hints are free. Stars mark the squares to reach; in those steps, and in the ones that ask to capture every piece or build a castle, the other side passes and the step has a number of moves: past them it starts again. A lesson is passed when every step is done without Skip, and the list marks it with ✓. Closing the drawer goes back to the list, and any other game ends the lesson.

## Mate problems

☰ → Mate problems… is tsume shōgi (詰将棋): a position with few pieces, where you play Sente and have to checkmate in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9 plies, every move giving check. The plies are the 手 of 3手詰; in English 3 plies is "Mate in 2", counting only your moves. The engine defends as long as it can.

Where there are problems of more than one variant (standard and Minishōgi), pick the variant first. Then pick the length and the problem from the grid of numbers and press Start. Solved problems have a ✓, which Clear solved forgets, and the grid starts at the first one of that length you have not solved yet. The defender's name says which problem is on the board ("Mate in 2, problem 12", the number of the grid).

A move that does not keep the mate goes back at once with its own sound and a vibration, and "Not the move" shows where the length was, as on Lishogi. The length ("Mate in 2") shortens with each move that keeps the mate. Hint shows the next move and Undo takes moves back; illegal moves are not played. Analyze Game is not offered in a problem. Solution is, in ☰ and in the notice when you solve it: the engine finds the whole line from the problem's position and shows it as moves, the defender's best resistance included. When you mate, the notice offers Next problem.

The problems follow tsume rules: the defender holds every piece not on the board or in your hand (so it can interpose), each of your moves is the only one that mates in time (the last may have twins), and no piece is left in your hand at the end. Standard shōgi's come from Generated Tsume-Shogi (Shinta Nakayama, CC BY 4.0), picked for their tactics (a sacrifice, a discovered check, a non-promotion; no gold dropped in front of the king), with a king added for Sente. Minishōgi's are generated by `tools/make-problems.py`.

## Sandbox

☰ → Sandbox sets up any position for any variant: pick a piece from the palette and tap squares, drag pieces, drag across empty squares to select several (then promote, flip or delete them), and move pieces to and from the hands. Any number of pieces of a kind is fine. The tools (Move, Erase, the color, who moves, the variant) are chips above the palette, with Promote, Flip side and Delete taking the hint's place while squares are selected, and the actions (Clear…, Initial position, Play, Cancel, Done) wide buttons under it, Done the main one. Play starts the position at once between two players; Done makes it the start of the next new game.

With the keyboard or a controller, the arrows or the d-pad move a cursor on the board and, past the top or bottom row, onto that hand. Select (Enter, the right button) taps there with the tool chosen; on a hand it puts the selected pieces in it, adds the palette's piece, or asks how many of each it holds. Cancel deselects. The hand button (Tab, Xbox X) puts the cursor on your hand, then the other's, then back on the board. LB/RB go through the palette, the hint button (H) changes its color, and LT/LS undo and redo. Browse the moves (M, RT) goes to the bar's buttons, where the arrows move, Select presses and Cancel goes back to the board.

## Online

- Network game: one device hosts and the other joins, on the same network (rooms are found on their own) or over the internet with the host's port open (Preferences → Online; UPnP can ask the router). While the room waits, the board shows the game to come (the host's variant and handicap). A tap on the settings in the lobby lets the host change them (variant, clock, handicap, sides, move rules, password) without hosting again; the other player then presses Ready again. Everything the two devices say is encrypted, and the room password never goes over the network. The host can set a room password, kick or ban, and both sides can chat, ask for a takeback and ask for a rematch.
- Takebacks: the other player's request shows in the bar, with Accept and Decline in place of its buttons, and playing a move declines it. After a decline, the next request waits for another move. Asking while the other player asks accepts theirs, and a lost connection drops a request. As on Lishogi, whoever gets a move back keeps the time they had after it, and the other player gets back the time of the turn they were on.
- Rematch: after a game the host can change the room's settings from the result notice's Room settings, without hosting again. The rematch starts with them, and a side the host picked there replaces the usual swap.
- A lost connection pauses the clocks and reconnects, and either player can close the game and open it again to go back to it (the joiner without typing the room password again).
- Lishogi: needs a Lishogi token (Preferences → Online, with a link to create it). The choices are under New Game. Lishogi lets one device at a time follow your game: opening it on another (the Kindle and the computer with the same account) moves it there, the first one says so, and a move or a chat line on it brings the game back.
- CSA server: a server address (Floodgate by default), login and password.

## Preferences

Preferences opens as a list of sections, each with what is chosen in it; a tap opens the section over the list (‹ goes back, × closes them all). A choice that opens a list (Players, Clock…) opens it the same way: a tap on a row shows that choice at once, Confirm keeps it, and Cancel, ‹ or × puts back the one before. Every other change counts at once, on the Kindle too. At the foot of each section (but Language and Controls, which have their own defaults, and Updates), Reset this section puts only its settings back to how the game was installed (it asks first). Under the sections, Reset everything puts the settings back to how the game was installed and clears the game history (it asks first); the game in progress, with the choices it was started with, and saved games stay.

- Game: show legal moves, illegal moves, automatic promotion and, on computers (not Android) the engine: the built-in one or another USI program (YaneuraOu, for one) for the standard game and its analysis. The variants keep the built-in engine, and the levels only work with a program that has a Skill Level option; the program runs in its own folder, where YaneuraOu looks for `eval/`.
- Pieces: the piece set (1 kanji UnGungseo by default, the list with each set's pieces), "Draw the piece shape" (on by default; off, the glyph alone fills its square, every piece the same size), "Colored pieces" (color screens: each kind of piece in its own color, the background or, without the shape, the glyph; on by default for Dōbutsu), "Show how pieces move", "Show the unpromoted side of a picked-up piece" (or of any promoted piece tapped, until a second tap), "Chess mode" (as in chess the first player white: sente's symbols white with a black outline and gote's black, all facing the same way, with the piece shape or without; the pictures inverted as chess sets draw their white and black pieces; the animals keep their pictures on white tiles for sente and black ones for gote; it takes the place of "Colored pieces", which is then locked, and both are locked for the animals without the shape) and notation (Japanese ▲７六歩 or Western ☗P-7f), for all variants.
- Variants: a row per variant, with its own piece set, piece shape, move marks, colors, chess mode and sound set, each "Same as all variants" or its own, the board showing that variant's start while its page is open (Dōbutsu and Goro Goro: the animals with their shape, move marks and colors, and Bubbles; Tori shōgi: Birds, chirps and whistled tunes). Tori shōgi offers only the sets with its birds of their own (1 kanji in Noto Sans JP, Zen Antique and AR PL UKai, the animals, and the pictorial set with birds drawn in its own style). The animal set has Tori shōgi's birds: the phoenix (Noto Emoji's, a bird in flames), the crane white with the red crown of the 丹頂 (on a flamingo's long legs: Unicode has no crane), the pheasant a cock in the copper pheasant's (ヤマドリ) colors, the quails small birds facing away from their side, the swallow navy in flight and the goose grey-brown, the falcon perched and the eagle with its wings spread.
- Appearance: colors (automatic, black and white, color), the theme of black and white screens, the palette of color ones (as swatches), wooden board, colors for color blindness (the last move in blue instead of green, the hint in vermilion instead of green), coordinates, the text size of menus, dialogs and chat (75–200%, − / +; the board always fills the window) and, on the Kindle, the screen (portrait, landscape, landscape upside down), the front light (the Kindle's own setting, − / +), clock updates and refreshing the whole screen after each move and a drawer as it opens (e-ink: black, then drawn again, so nothing ghosts; both off by default), and turning pages in long lists (also on Android, for e-ink devices); on Android, "Use the space beside the camera" lays the game out under a notch or punch hole too (off by default: it keeps clear of it).
- Sound: the sound set (silent by default; Pachi, Soft, Wood, Bubbles, Koto, Retro, Birds, Clappers (拍子木, the kabuki clappers, which open a game as in the theatre), Bamboo (竹筒, a hollow bamboo tube struck with a stick) and Taiko (太鼓, the big drum, with the small tight one, 締太鼓, on check); ▶ plays it; each has its own sounds for the start, a check, the move that wins on the board (checkmate, the lion's try), a mate problem's wrong move, the end, the chat and the last seconds of the clock, in the same timbre), the volume (− / +, ten at a time) and Vibration (on by default; not on the Kindle): moves, captures, checks, a mate problem's wrong move and the game's end are felt on the controller while it is in use (Linux evdev, Windows XInput) and on the phone otherwise, and a short tick answers each button and tap on the phone and each press of the controller.
- Language: automatic follows the system.
- Online: your name, the port when hosting, UPnP and the Lishogi token.
- Updates (where the game looks for them): see Updates.
- Controls (computers): see Playing.

## Updates

The first time, as soon as the board shows (before the tutorial's question), the game asks whether to check GitHub for a new version each time it starts: it can update itself, tell you, or not check; Preferences → Updates changes it (Automatic, Tell me, Off), shows the version and the build, whether the game is up to date with a button to look again, update or restart, and links to the releases on GitHub. Updating automatically works on the Kindle, the Linux folder, the AppImage and the Windows portable folder, when it can be written: the new version is downloaded, checked against the release's signature and put in place of the old one, which comes back if the new one fails to start; settings, games and your own sets stay. The game starts again at once if no game is in progress, else the new version starts the next time (About → Restart). On Android the game downloads and checks it and hands it to the system, which asks before it installs (choosing Automatic or Tell me first opens Android's page to allow installs from Denboku); the Windows MSIX and the Flatpak do not update themselves: install the new one from the release (the MSIX goes over the old one, keeping its settings). About shows the same, with a link to the game's page on GitHub (on the Kindle, the address to read).

## Streaming and recording

The piece set list and the file chooser's own windows open apart from the game's window, so OBS's window capture (and other per-window captures) show only the board. Use display capture (Screen Capture on Linux, Display Capture on Windows), cropped to the game if needed.

### Your own piece and sound sets

They go in the `custom/` folder: `custom/pieces/` and `custom/sounds/` in the data folder (the first row above), on the Kindle `extensions/denboku/custom/` of its drive, on Android `Android/data/jp.denboku/files/custom/` of the phone's storage (a computer sees both over USB). ☰ → Help → How to Play → Your pieces and sounds shows where it is on the device in use, with Open Folder on computers. On computers Preferences → Game picks another folder for them, at once (Default folder goes back). Restart the game and the sets appear in Preferences beside the built-in ones. A set missing something falls back to a built-in one, so a set made today keeps working when a later version adds pieces or sounds.

- Pieces as pictures: a folder or a zip in `custom/pieces/` with a PNG per piece named by its code, `FU` (pawn), `KY`, `KE`, `GI`, `KI`, `KA`, `HI`, `OU`, `GY` (the kings; either stands in for the other), `TO`, `NY`, `NK`, `NG`, `UM`, `RY`, as `FU.png` or pychess's `0FU.png` (sente) and `1FU.png` (gote; without it sente's is turned around). Folders inside the zip do not matter. Sets drawn as SVG (pychess's, Ka-hu's) have to be converted to PNG first, for instance with `rsvg-convert -h 256 0FU.svg -o 0FU.png`. A `style.ini` beside the pictures can give the name (`Name=`, `Name[ja]=`) and `NotFor=`; without it the set is named after the folder or zip. A picture is the whole piece, so the piece shape, colors, chess mode and move marks do not change it; a piece without a picture (Tori shōgi's birds) is drawn with the plain kanji set's.
- Pieces from a font: a file `custom/pieces/<name>.ini` written as the ones in `data/styles/` (`Name=`, `Layout=`, `Font=`…), with its fonts (OTF or TTF) beside it. A piece the font has no glyph for is drawn with the plain kanji set's.
- Sounds: a folder `custom/sounds/<name>/` with 16-bit WAV files named as in `data/sounds/2-Pachi/` (`move.wav`, `capture.wav`, `check.wav`…); any file left out is played from the first built-in set. A name starting with a number and a dash (`2-Mine`) sorts first and shows without the prefix.

On Android the files are in the app's private folder; Save Game and Save as CSA also copy the game to Documents/Denboku (Android 10 and later), where the file manager and other apps see it.

`settings.ini` holds the preferences, `autosave.kif` the game in progress and `history/` the finished games (KIF, named by their start time). The Flatpak keeps them in its own data directory. Preferences → Game → Data folder (on computers) picks another folder for all of them, your own piece and sound sets included: it is used from the next start, and the files of the current folder are copied to it then if it has no `settings.ini` yet; the choice is kept in a `datafolder` file in the default folder, and Default folder undoes it. `DENBOKU_HOME` moves all of them elsewhere and `DENBOKU_ENGINE` picks another USI engine for everything, over the Preferences.
