# Code

How the program is put together, and the formats other versions and programs depend on. Details stay in the comments at the top of each file and above each function; this page is the map. Building is in [`BUILD.md`](BUILD.md), the look and the Kindle constraints in [`DESIGN.md`](DESIGN.md).

## Layers

C11 and GTK 2.20 / GLib 2.29 (the Kindle's own libraries, so no newer API), Cairo and FreeType for drawing.

`src/core/` is the app, and knows the game only through `src/shogi/rules.h` (with `online.h` and `texts.h` there); `src/shogi/` is the game. See "Another game" below.

| Layer | Files | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| The game (`src/shogi/`) | `shogi.c` (positions, moves, SFEN, variants, perft), `game_rules.c` (handicaps, how games end, impasse, the engine's position text), `kif.c` (KIF with board diagrams, CSA), `notation.c` (Japanese and Western move text, coordinates), `setup.c` (Sandbox pieces and checks, hand order), `piece_art.c`, `strings.c` (its texts and translations) | C only, tested by `make test` |
| Records | `game.c` (a game: moves, times, result), `json.c` | the game |
| Board | `layout.c` (where everything on the game screen goes for a window's size, and the size that gives the board and the moves all they take: arithmetic only, tested over a grid of sizes by `make test`), `view.c` (Cairo drawing, the bar of actions, taps, premoves, arrows, editing, an overlay drawn over it all), `sheet.c` (a sheet of tiles as that overlay: layout, drawing, taps, drags, keys), `pieces.c` (piece styles, fonts) | GTK, the game |
| Outside world | `engine.c` (USI or UCI), `proc.c` (child programs, line by line, without blocking), `net.c` (LAN TCP and UDP discovery), `netcrypt.c` (the LAN's encryption), `netproto.c` (what a LAN line says, its numbers and texts within limits), `upnp.c`, `http.c` (HTTPS through the `curl` program), `update.c` (the update manifest: Ed25519 signature, BLAKE2b-256 of a package, build order; tested by `make test`), `i18n.c` | GLib |
| The program | the files sharing `app.h`, below | everything |

The program is one global `App app` (`app.h`) and one file per area:

- `app.c`: startup, the window, the callbacks of the view's bar of actions, rotation, resume of the last game.
- `play.c`: turns, clocks, the engine's moves, hints, game end, autosave.
- `controls.c` (the actions and their keys and controller buttons, the glyphs drawn beside the buttons on screen, Preferences → Controls), `gamepad.c` (controllers: evdev on Linux, XInput and winmm on Windows, what the app reads on Android; the inputs are positions, A the bottom face button, and the layout comes from the driver or the name).
- `analysis.c`: game analysis with a second engine at full strength, Lishogi's marks (Advice.scala).
- `menus.c`: the ☰ menu (its pages of tiles and their icons, shown with `sheet.c`), New Game, Load Game and Game History, How to Play, About, undo.
- `prefs.c`: Preferences, a page of sections and a page per section, each change applied at once; `rows.c` builds those pages' rows (cards, switches, chips, choices that open a list, − / +, sections with their summary).
- `records.c`: loading records (files, clipboard), replay, the game history (its index read and written by `histindex.c`, no GTK).
- `lan.c` (network games), `chat.c` (the chat of network and Lishogi games: bubbles, the panel at the left in landscape or above the board in portrait); in `src/shogi/`, `lishogi.c` (Board API, opening a Lishogi game), `lishogi_setup.c` (what Lishogi accepts when a game is asked of it: its lists of times, when a game may be rated, the request's form; `online_setup.h`, tested without GTK by `tests/test_online.c`) and `csa.c` (CSA servers), as `online_*` and `server_*` (`online.h`).
- `editor.c` (Sandbox), `sound.c` (with `mixer.c`: a new sound takes over from the one playing, which fades out, instead of queueing behind it), `theme.c` (piece styles per variant, palettes, color modes), `style.c` (GTK's default drawing replaced by flat rounded buttons, fields, tabs and check boxes; the drawers' rows and buttons at least a finger tall, 48 of a computer's pixels scaled by the text, chips 42), `dialogs.c` (dialogs that work on the Kindle), `drawer.c` (those dialogs shown as drawers in the window, over the game or beside it, in a container of its own that keeps them next to the game's column: GtkDialog's show and hide replaced for them, drop-downs mirrored as chips or a list, label-and-control tables stacked), `settings.c` (paths, `settings.ini`; the data folder the player chose, from the `datafolder` file in the default one, with `DENBOKU_HOME` over it and `DENBOKU_DEV`'s `dev/` profile; the flags are in [`BUILD.md`](BUILD.md#developer-flags)), `kindle.c` (save before the lock screen, redraw after it), `zip.c` (reading zips: picture piece sets, the Windows update), `updater.c` (updates: the first question, the daily look at the latest release's `update.txt` and `update.txt.sig`, the download and the swap of the game's own files where it can, About's line; the address and the public keys are the game's, `src/shogi/release.h`), `android.c` (the link to the Android app: input, redraws, screen size, sounds; see [`ANDROID.md`](ANDROID.md)).

## Flow of a game

- `app.game` (`Game`, `game.h`) is the whole state: start position, moves, times per move, result. Everything else (the board, the move list, the clocks, the files) is drawn or written from it.
- The `View` draws `app.game` and reports through `ViewCallbacks` (`view.h`): `move` when a move is made on the board, `can_move`/`can_premove` to ask whose pieces may move, `player_text` for each player's name and clock, `ply_changed` when the move list is browsed, and `edit_tap`/`edit_event` in the Sandbox. The callbacks live in `play.c` and `editor.c`.
- `play_move()` plays a move for whoever made it, sends it out (LAN, Lishogi or CSA), and calls `next_turn()`. `next_turn()` is the one place that follows every move: it redraws, schedules the autosave, updates Undo and Hint, starts the engine or the premove for the side to move, and when the game has a result shows the game-over dialog (and stores the game in the history).
- Nothing blocks the main loop: the engine, `curl` and sockets deliver lines through GLib watches (`proc.c`, `net.c`) to callbacks, which act from idle handlers when they need to open a dialog.
- Rules have one implementation, in C, in `src/shogi/`; every other part asks it through `rules.h`.

## LAN protocol

TCP on port 4081 (Preferences → Online can change the host's port; a joiner may give `host:port`). UTF-8 lines ending in `\n`. Either side ignores lines it does not know, so a newer version can talk to an older one; a line that needs both sides to understand it bumps `NET_VERSION` in `app.h` (now 5; every peer past the key exchange is 4 or later) and is only sent when the other side's `DENBOKU` line says so.

Every line is encrypted (`netcrypt.c`, on Monocypher in `src/third_party/`). On connecting each side sends, in clear, `KEY <its X25519 key, hex>`, a fresh one per connection; the host's ends in ` 1` when the room has a password. The session key is BLAKE2b of the shared secret, both keys and, with a password, Argon2id of it (1 MiB, 3 passes, salted with both keys). From then on each line is base64 of the Poly1305 tag and the XChaCha20 ciphertext, the nonce being the direction and the line's number, so that a line changed, sent again or out of order closes the connection. The password never goes over the network: the joiner's first sealed line, `OK`, shows the host it has the key. Until then the joiner is kept apart at the door, and the player is not disturbed: one waits per address, four at most, so that a stranger connecting again and again does not take the place of the player coming back. A side that answers `KEY` with anything else is an older version: the joiner says so and gives up. The CSA server (`csa.c`) uses the same connection in clear (`net_join_plain()`).

Discovery: a waiting host broadcasts `DENBOKU-HOST 2 <game port> <name>` over UDP to port 4081 every two seconds; the name carries the room's settings as numbers joined by tabs (`<name> <password 0/1> <variant> <clock choice> <main s> <byoyomi s> <increment s> <set-up 0/1> <handicap>`, `net_room_text()` and `net_room_parse()` in `netproto.c`), which each joiner writes in its own language (an announcement of the name alone is shown as it is), and the joiner's list shows each host it hears. The same announcement also goes out as an mDNS response (`mdns.c`, service `_denboku._udp.local`: PTR, SRV with the game port and a TXT `name=`) to 224.0.0.251:5353 from every interface, and the joiner listens there too, for routers that drop the subnet broadcast but pass multicast; a host heard both ways, or from two of its addresses (cable and Wi-Fi: the same name and port), is one entry.

| Line | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `NAME <name>` | both | the player's name, first thing on connecting |
| `DENBOKU <version>` | both | the protocol version |
| `DENIED` | host | in clear: wrong password (a first line that does not open); the host drops the connection |
| `GAME <handicap> <clock> <joiner's color> <move rule>` | host | the game's settings: handicap index, clock preset index (the last is custom), 0 sente / 1 gote, legal-move rule |
| `CLOCK <main s> <byoyomi s> <increment s>` | host | the custom clock (version 2) |
| `VARIANT <n>` | host | the variant index (version 2) |
| `SFEN [<sfen>]` | host | the start position from the Sandbox, empty for the usual one |
| `READY 1` / `READY 0` | joiner | ready in the lobby |
| `MOVES <sente ms> <gote ms> <ms used this turn> <move>…` | host | starts the game, or sends the whole game again after a reconnection |
| `+7776FU`, `-3334FU`… | both | a move in CSA notation |
| `%TORYO` | both | resign |
| `%TIME_UP` | both | its own player's time ran out (version 5): each side decides on its own clock only, the other's being a round trip behind |
| `%ILLEGAL [<move>]` | both | an illegal move that loses (when the rules allow playing them) |
| `TAKEBACK` / `TAKEBACK 1` / `TAKEBACK 0` | both | asks to take back the last move; accepted or declined (a move declines it); declined, the same move gets `TAKEBACK 0` at once |
| `REMATCH` | both | asks for a rematch; the host starts it with `MOVES` |
| `CHAT <text>` | both | a chat line |
| `KICKED` / `BANNED` | host | the joiner was removed (banned addresses are told `BANNED` in clear on connecting) |
| `BYE` | host | the host took a newer connection for the same game, once it showed the key |
| `PING` | both | every 15 s; 45 s of silence counts as a lost connection |

A line longer than 64 KiB closes the connection, and the fifth wrong password from an address bans it from the room. A connection kept apart has 90 s and 4 KiB to show the key (the joiner types the password meanwhile). In the lobby the host sends `GAME`, `VARIANT`, `CLOCK` and `SFEN` again when its settings change, and a joiner that was ready drops it (`READY 0`). With a game going on, the joiner takes none that the host sends again. A lost connection does not end the game: the clocks stop, the host listens again, the joiner retries every 3 s, and the host's `MOVES` puts the game back. The joiner keeps the host's address in `settings.ini` (`LastHost`), so it can come back after a restart too; so does the host, from the autosave and the room kept in `[Game]` (`NetColor`, `NetRule`, `Handicap`, `NetStart` and `RoomPassword`, the room password, kept only while the hosted game lasts). The host sends the game settings once the joiner's `DENBOKU` line arrives (2 s at most).

## Files

- KIF (`game_write_kif`): standard KIF with 開始日時, 手合割 or a BOD board diagram, 先手/後手 names, times per move and the ending (投了, 詰み…). Variants add `# DENBOKU VARIANT <n>` and `# SFEN <start>` comments, which other programs skip; their moves are written as ordinary KIF on the variant's board.
- CSA (`game_write_csa`): CSA V2.2; variants add `'DENBOKU VARIANT <n>`.
- `settings.ini` (GKeyFile, readable by its user only outside Windows: it keeps `CsaPassword` and `LishogiToken`): `[Settings]` holds the preferences and last choices (each key is read where it is used, with `setting_int`/`setting_string` and its default); per variant (Preferences → Variants) are `Pieces.<Variant>`, `PieceShape.<Variant>`, `MoveMarks.<Variant>`, `ColoredPieces.<Variant>` and `SoundSet.<Variant>`, where an empty value or -1 means "same as all variants" and an absent one the variant's default (`variant_*_default()` in `rules.h`). `Key.<Action>` and `Pad.<Action>` (`Up`, `Select`, `Hint`… as in `ACTIONS[]`, `controls.c`) hold two keys (GTK accelerator names, `<Control>z`) or two controller inputs (`A`, `DPadUp`, `StickLeft`…) joined by `;`; absent means the default. `PieceShape` (1 the koma or tile, the default; 0 the glyph alone; `text.ini`, the old text-only set, reads as `kanji1.ini`), `ColoredPieces` and `ColoredPieces.<Variant>` (as the move marks; 1 each piece in its color on color screens; absent, on for whoever had one of the colored sets, which read as their plain set: `GONE_SETS` in `theme.c`), `TextSize` (percent, `apply_text_size()` in `theme.c`), `Engine` (0 built-in, 1 the program in `EnginePath`, for the standard game only; `engine_path()` in `play.c`), `AnalysisTime`, `AnalysisThreads`, `AnalysisMemory`, `AnalysisLines`, `AnalysisArrows` and `AnalysisBoardMarks` are the analysis options. `OnlineTimeMode`, `OnlineLimit`, `OnlineByoyomi`, `OnlineIncrement` (seconds), `OnlinePeriods`, `OnlineDays`, `OnlineBelow` and `OnlineAbove` (rating points) are the last Lishogi choices. `PadLayout` picks the controller's names (0 automatic, 1 Xbox, 2 Nintendo, 3 PlayStation) and `ButtonLegend` 0 hides the glyphs. `[Game]` describes the game in progress (mode, variant, engine sides, level, clock and remaining time), next to `autosave.kif`.
- Updates: `update.txt` (`tools/release.sh`) is `[Release]` `Build`, `Version`, `Tag` (the release's tag: the packages are downloaded from `releases/download/<Tag>/`, not from `latest/`, which may have moved on since the manifest was read), `Notes` and a group per platform (`kindle`, `linux-x86_64`, `linux-arm64`, `appimage-x86_64`, `windows-x86_64`, `android`, `android-x86_64`) with `File`, `Size` and `Blake2b` (hex); `tools/release.sh` refuses a package whose `<file>.build` (written by `build.sh`) is another build. `update.txt.sig` is its Ed25519 signature (hex) by one of the keys in `release.h`. The Kindle and the Linux folder unpack the package beside the game (`denboku-update/`) and rename each of its entries over the game's, keeping the game's own in `old/` with a `.update-pending` mark written before the first rename (a swap cut short is undone like a failed start); the game removes both once a run that found the mark has lasted 30 s, and the start script (`kindle/launch.sh`, which also keeps each run's exit status in `exits.log`, and `linux/denboku.sh`) puts `old/` back when a run that started with the mark fails by itself (not when killed: SIGHUP, SIGINT, SIGKILL, SIGTERM), renaming the mark `.update-failed` once all of `old/` is back, after which the game sets `UpdateSkip` to that build (kept in `update-build` of the data folder) and does not take it again. The AppImage is replaced by renaming, the old one kept as `.old` until then; nothing puts it back by itself (rename it by hand). On Android the APK, downloaded into the data folder and checked, goes to the system's `PackageInstaller` (`install` to the app, `MainActivity.install()`), which asks the player. On Windows a file in use cannot be replaced: the game unpacks the zip into `update\` (with `zip.c`) and writes the folder's name in `update\ready`, and `Denboku.exe` (`windows/launcher.c`) moves it in, the replaced files into `old\`, before it starts the game, and moves them back if the game fails within 15 s, at any start while the mark is there; an update that comes while the one before is untried waits. `Updates` in `settings.ini`: 0 or absent not asked yet, 1 install by itself, 2 tell me, 3 off; `UpdateChecked` the last look (seconds since 1970).
- Lessons, `data/lessons/lessons*.txt`: a line per item, `#` for comments. `chapter <title>` starts a chapter; `lesson <id> <title>` a lesson (the id is what `LessonsDone` in `settings.ini` keeps, the passed ones, comma separated; an `id:n` of the old stars counts as passed) with `about <subtitle>`; `step <title>` a step of it, with `text <paragraph>` (repeated for more), `position <the game's text of a position>` (the side to move is the player), `goal <kind>`, `limit <moves>` (the most where the other side passes; past them the step starts again), `hint <move>`, `done <text>` (instead of "Well done!") and `solution <moves>` (only for the tests). The goals: `stars <squares>` (land on each, the other side passing), `capture` (take every piece of the other side, which passes), `shape <position>` (the player's pieces of that position where it has them, the other side passing), `line <moves>` (the player's and the replies by turns; `a/b` for the player's alternatives; `move` is the same word), `mate [n]` (checkmate in n moves, one by default, every move giving check as in tsume; the other side answers with the defence that lasts longest, found by a small search in `lessons.c`, and a check that lets the king go is a wrong move), `check` (a move that gives check), `quiz <squares>` (tap one of the squares, as reading a square's name or a piece's kanji; nothing moves; the view hands taps to it through `square_tap`), `brink` (必至: a quiet move after which every defence loses to a mate in one, the other side then playing the defence that leaves the fewest mates, and that mate) and `any` (any legal move). Texts are English and translated in `strings_<code>.c`; `make test` plays every step.
- `history/`: finished games as KIF named `YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.kif` after their start, the last 200. `index.txt` (`histindex.c`) keeps what the Game History list shows, so that opening it reads only the files new or changed since: a line `denboku-history 1`, then a line per game with these fields joined by tabs: file name, its modification time (seconds since 1970) and size, the start (seconds since 1970), variant, result, reason, number of moves, and the two players' names. A file whose time or size differs is read again, entries of files gone drop out, and a missing or damaged index is rebuilt.
- Piece styles, `data/styles/*.ini`: `[Style]` with `Name` (and `Name[ja]`, `Name[pt]`), `Font`, `Layout` (1 or 2 kanji), `GlyphScale` (on the koma), `GlyphY` (the glyph's center, 63 by default), `KeepGlyphSize` (the glyph does not shrink with a smaller koma), `Pixels` (pixel art on a grid that many pixels wide), `PixelFont` (with `Pixels`: the font's em in pixels, drawn one font pixel per art pixel), `Pictures`, `FitEach`, `Layers` (the font of the `[Layers]` glyphs), `Shape=square`, `MarkColor`, `Variant` (offered only for that variant), `NotFor` (not offered for these variants); `[Text]`, `[Fonts]`, `[Scale]` (one piece's glyph larger or smaller on the koma; without the piece shape every glyph is fitted to the same size), `[Fill]` (the colors with Colored pieces; without it, `PIECE_ART`'s) and `[Layers]` (`U+E000 #rrggbb, …`: glyphs painted in those colors under the piece's glyph on color screens, or over it with `top` after the color) set one piece each by its CSA code; `[Mirror]` draws those pieces' pictures facing the other way. `piece_side()`'s character (Tori shōgi's 左 and 右) goes on a red seal in kanji sets, in Noto Sans JP whatever the set, and not with the move marks; sets of pictures tell the side by which way the bird faces.

## Recipes

- **A visible text**: write it in English inside `tr("…")` and add its translation to each language's table: `src/core/lang_<code>.c`, or `src/shogi/strings_<code>.c` when it is about shōgi; a missing translation shows the English. Long texts (How to Play, About) are macros in `src/shogi/texts.h`.
- **A language**: a row in `LANGUAGES` (`i18n.c`: its code, as in locales and the style files' `Name[xx]`, a regional one such as `zh_TW` when needed, and its name in itself) with the app's table in `src/core/lang_<code>.c`, and a row in `GAME_LANGUAGES` (`src/shogi/strings.c`) with the game's table in `src/shogi/strings_<code>.c`; both tables end with `{NULL, NULL}`. Preferences, the system language and the piece set names follow. Characters outside the bundled fonts (Noto Sans JP has Latin, Greek, Cyrillic and Japanese) need a font in `data/fonts/` and `tools/make-ui-font.py` (through `tools/make-fonts.sh`).
- **A preference**: a row on its section's page (`prefs.c`): `add_switch(card, label, key, value)`, `add_choice()` for a list, `row_chips()` or `row_stepper()` with a callback that calls `set_int()`; it is applied in `apply_settings()` and read with `setting_int(key, default)` where it matters. Then the manual.
- **A variant**: its rules in `VARIANTS[]` (`shogi.c`), its name in `variant_name()` and `VARIANT_LABELS`, its rules text in `VARIANT_RULES` (`strings.c`), its Fairy-Stockfish name in `variant_engine_name()` and any SFEN differences in `game_engine_position()` (`game_rules.c`), its pieces in `setup.c`, and perft and record tests in `tests/`.
- **A piece set**: an `.ini` in `data/styles/` with its font in `data/fonts/` (cut to the needed glyphs with `tools/font-subset.sh`, added to `tools/make-fonts.sh`).
- **A menu entry**: a line in `MENU[]` or one of its pages (`FILES_MENU[]`…, `menus.c`) and its icon in `draw_icon()`; `pc` entries are left out on the Kindle, `optional` ones while they cannot be used.
- **A LAN line**: send it only when `app.peer_version` is new enough, read it in `net_parse()` (`netproto.c`, with its limits and a case in `tests/test_netproto.c`), act on it in `net_line_cb()` (`lan.c`), and add it to the table above.

## Another game

A fork for another game (chess, say) keeps `src/core/` and writes `src/<game>/`, built with `make GAME=<game>` (the Makefile takes `-Isrc/core -Isrc/$(GAME)` and every `.c` in both). `src/<game>/` provides:

- `rules.h`: the names `src/core/` uses. `Position`, `Move` (`from`, `to`, `promote`, `piece`, `captured`), `PIECE`/`TYPE`/`COLOR`, `SQ`/`ROW`/`COL`, `board_w`/`board_h`/`SQUARES`, `FIRST`/`SECOND`, `KING`, `V_STANDARD`/`V_COUNT`, `rules_set_variant()` and `pos_*` (legal moves, make/unmake, check, `pos_to_text()`/`pos_from_text()`), `square_from_text()` and `move_problem()` (why a move is not allowed); `game_style()` (the strategies of a game as players name them, shown in ⓘ; `src/shogi/style.c`); hands (`hand`, `hand_slot()`, `hand_order()`, `DROP`; empty for a game without drops); piece art (`PIECE_ART`, `PIECE_KINDS`, `piece_kind()` and its inverse `kind_type()`: a variant may draw a type as a kind of its own, as Tori shōgi's birds; `piece_side()`, the small 左 or 右 of its quails); handicaps (odds games); `game_judge()` for how a game ends; the engine's `ENGINE_PROTOCOL` (`"uci"` for chess) and `game_engine_position()`; records (`record_read`/`record_write`, PGN for chess, an `_alt` format, `record_read_any_text()` for pasted text, the extensions and their labels), LAN move text (`move_to_net`/`move_from_net`), the Sandbox (`setup_*`), the clock presets and side labels of New Game; the sides' names, marks (`SIDE_MARKS`, `SIDE_SYMBOLS`) and texts, why a game ended (`reason_text()`), the promotion question's answers, the board's star points and its squares' proportions (`SQUARE_RATIO`, `BOARD_USUAL`), and the texts of the declaration and the mate problems.
- `notation.h`: `move_notation()`, `file_label()`/`rank_label()`, and a sample move in each notation (`NOTATION_SAMPLES`, `NOTATION_WIDEST`).
- `online.h`: an online server (Lishogi's Board API is lichess's, so `lishogi.c` mostly needs its address and the variant and move spellings) and a second server or stubs; their labels. `online_setup.h` holds the choices of a game asked of the server (`OnlineSetup`), the lists New Game offers and the server's rules on them.
- `release.h`: where updates come from and the public keys that sign them (`updater.c`).
- `texts.h` and `strings.c`: How to Play, About, the variants' names and rules, and the game's translations.
- Mate problems, in `src/core/` (a fork writes only the data): `problems.c` (the list, "<plies> <position>" per line in `data/problems/problems.txt` for standard shōgi and `problems-<engine's variant name>.txt` for another variant, each problem carrying its variant, and which are solved, no GTK), `problem.c` (the dialog), and in `play.c` the flow: the problem starts as a game from its position with Sente human and the engine at full strength as the defender; after each attacker move the defender's search score (`score mate -k`, in plies) says whether the mate is kept, else the move is taken back; the attacker's moves still to make come from the move count. Solution (`problem_solution`) searches the problem's start position and writes the last mate `pv` as moves with `move_notation`. `tools/pick-problems.py` picks standard shōgi's from a dataset and `tools/make-problems.py` generates Minishōgi's from random positions (both in [`BUILD.md`](BUILD.md#assets)).
- Lessons (the tutorial), in `src/core/` too: `lessons.c` reads `data/lessons/lessons.txt` (standard) and `lessons-<engine's variant name>.txt` and plays a step (no GTK; `tests/test_lessons.c` plays every step's solution), `tutorial.c` is the list and the lesson's drawer. Their positions are `pos_from_text()` text, their squares `square_from_text()` and their moves `game_move_from_engine()`'s, so a chess fork writes its own lessons in its own notation; a wrong move is explained by `move_problem()`. The format is under "Lessons" in Files.
- `data/`: piece sets (`data/styles/*.ini` name pieces by the codes in `PIECE_ART`; a folder or zip of PNGs named by those codes is a set too, read by `pieces.c` with `zip.c`), fonts, sounds, art. The user's own sets are read from `custom/pieces/` and `custom/sounds/` of the data folder (`app.custom`; on Android `DENBOKU_CUSTOM`, a folder a computer sees over USB) beside these (`style_path()` in `theme.c`, `sound_path()` in `sound.c`): a style takes its fonts from its own folder first, a sound a set lacks comes from the first built-in set.

What still says shōgi in `src/core/`: the program's name and paths (`denboku`, `DENBOKU_*`, `settings.c`, `kindle.c`), the promotion question (promote or not; chess picks a piece) in `view.c`, and the layout's piece stands (komadai), which a game without hands leaves empty.
